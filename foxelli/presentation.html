Creative Strategy

My Creative Approach

🎭

Brand Storytelling

Each piece of content was designed to feel like it came from a real Mrs. Quilty subscriber — not an ad. Warm, unhurried, and deeply personal to the craft community.

🧵

Character Continuity

Three distinct mini-narratives (Owl story, Frog story, Unboxing journey) each with recurring characters to create emotional resonance across the video series.

📸

UGC Authenticity

Images were prompted to mimic real iPhone photography — imperfect exposure, natural lighting, lived-in homes, and visible human imperfections. No studio aesthetics.

🌅

Lighting Variety

Soft morning bedroom light, warm fireplace glow, bright kitchen noon light, shaded afternoon — covering the full emotional range of the brand world.

💡

Prompt Engineering

Prompts were hyper-specific: exact fabric patterns, precise body language, camera perspective, lighting source, and emotional tone — for maximum consistency.

🎬

Image-to-Video Pipeline

Lifestyle stills generated in Midjourney & Freepik AI were used as start frames in Kling AI and Runway — giving the video tool a visual anchor before adding motion prompts.

Workflow

AI Tools & Workflow

Midjourney Image Generation
Used for lifestyle image generation where fine control over composition, lighting, and character detail was needed. Reference images were assigned specific roles — one for environment/lighting, one for character consistency, one for product colors and fabric details — keeping results coherent across the series without starting from scratch each time.
→ Part 1 lifestyle images · Character reference · Environment framing
Freepik AI Image Generation
Used for realistic UGC-style stills and as start-frame sources for video generation. Prompts were structured as unified scenes (composition + character + product + atmosphere) rather than separate fields. A fixed set of realism cues — "shot on iPhone", "slight film grain", "candid", "nothing staged" — was included in every prompt to steer output away from stock-photo aesthetics.
→ UGC-feel stills · Video start frames · Edit mode for targeted changes
Kling AI Video Generation
Primary video tool. All clips were built on top of generated stills used as start frames — giving Kling a concrete visual anchor rather than asking it to construct a scene from text alone. Motion prompts were written in three layers: action, pace, and emotional register. Hands-only framing was a deliberate strategy: it increases authenticity, invites viewer identification, and plays to Kling's strengths in hand and object movement.
→ Image-to-video animation · Hands & craft motion clips · Narrative sequences
Runway Video Generation
Used alongside Kling for video clips requiring different motion characteristics — particularly useful for wider shots and camera movement. Combined with Adobe After Effects for final assembly, color grading, and sequencing of individual clips into cohesive video stories.
→ Additional video clips · Camera motion · Final post-production in After Effects

Prompt Strategy

How the Prompts Were Built

Core Approach

Every piece of content was built around one question: "does this look like something a real person filmed?" UGC authenticity wasn't a stylistic preference — it was the production brief. That meant actively working against the polished, symmetrical, over-lit results AI tools tend to default to, and deliberately steering toward imperfection at every step.

Still Image Production (Freepik AI)

Prompts structured as a unified scene — the way a photographer would brief a shoot. Reference images assigned specific roles: environment/lighting, character consistency, product/fabric detail. A fixed set of realism cues in every prompt: "shot on iPhone", "slight film grain", "candid", "nothing staged", "realistic skin texture", "not airbrushed". In edit mode, short and binary — only describing what changes, never overwriting stable elements.

Video Production (Kling AI)

All videos built on top of generated stills as start frames — giving Kling a concrete visual anchor. Motion prompts written in three layers: action, pace, and emotional register. "Gently slides across the surface", "slow and deliberate", "a moment of hesitation before opening" — each phrase carries both physical and tonal information at once. Faces kept out of frame deliberately: hands-only framing increases authenticity and plays to Kling's actual strengths.

Narrative Structure — Video 3

Written as a three-act sequence, not a collection of shots: box picked up from doorstep → hand explores the lid, hesitates → lid opens, fabrics revealed. Each prompt written so the emotional logic carries from one shot to the next. The result is content that works as individual clips and holds together as a story when edited in sequence.

Process Notes

My Workflow & Reflections

🔍 Brand Research First

  • Studied Mrs. Quilty's brand identity, target audience (women 45–70, passionate crafters), and visual language before writing a single prompt
  • Identified core brand values: warmth, authenticity, community, the joy of making things by hand
  • Avoided polished or commercial aesthetics — leaned into imperfection as a feature, not a bug
  • Reference imagery informed the setting details: specific fabric patterns (blue floral, polka dot, gingham)

✍️ Prompt Engineering — Images

  • Each prompt written as a single unified scene (composition + character + product + atmosphere), not a form to fill out
  • Reference images assigned specific roles: one for environment/lighting, one for character consistency, one for fabric/product details
  • Fixed realism cues in every prompt: "shot on iPhone", "slight film grain", "candid", "nothing staged", "realistic skin texture", "not airbrushed"
  • In Freepik edit mode, prompts kept deliberately short and binary — only describing what changes, never overwriting stable elements

🎬 Prompt Engineering — Video

  • Generated stills used as start frames in Kling AI and Runway — environment, lighting, and character established before a single video frame was generated
  • Motion prompts written in three layers: action, pace, and emotional register ("gently slides", "slow and deliberate", "a moment of hesitation")
  • Hands-only framing was a deliberate UGC strategy: increases authenticity, invites identification, and avoids face inconsistency risk across clips
  • Video 3 written as a three-act sequence — each prompt carries the emotional logic from the previous shot, works alone or as a series

💡 Key Creative Decisions

  • Three narrative arcs instead of three separate clips — builds emotional connection and watches to the end
  • The cat clip in Video 2 introduces levity — Mrs. Quilty is cozy, not precious. Relatable, shareable content
  • First-person POV for Video 3 puts the viewer inside the unboxing moment — maximum subscription box appeal
  • Consistent character design across each video series creates the feel of a real person's content feed
